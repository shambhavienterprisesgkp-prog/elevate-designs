The story

A Gilbert kid who went looking
for a decent croissant.

  • Gilbert, Arizona
    Grew up five minutes from where the shop sits today. Phoenix Country Day School, then east to Boston.
  • Tufts University
    A film degree — not a culinary one. Came home, couldn't find a croissant worth eating, and got quietly obsessed with fixing that.
  • France
    In 2022 he did what almost no Gilbert kid does: moved to France to learn pastry properly, the long way, in real boutiques.
  • Dubai — Burj Al Arab
    Cooked at one of the most demanding kitchens in the world, then trained in the orbit of Cédric Grolet, the pastry chef everyone else measures against.
  • Home again
    Came back to Gilbert with everything he'd learned and a single mission: bake the thing he couldn't find here.

"This is a desert bakery. These are pastries built to survive the heat — and to taste like Paris anyway."

The craft

We import the flour from one
mill in France. On purpose.

The flour comes from a single French mill called Antoine — grain that had never legally entered the United States before. There was FDA paperwork. Customs. A shipping container that had to fill before the boat could even leave port.

Then the real work: American eggs, Arizona humidity, and a recipe that had to be retuned from scratch. Twenty-four to thirty dough tests before the first one was good enough to sell. If a batch isn't right, it doesn't go in the case.
